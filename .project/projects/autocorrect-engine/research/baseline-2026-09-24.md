# Autocorrect baseline, 2026-09-24

## Summary

Current autocorrect almost never fires. With default settings it corrected 0 of 2,280 synthetic touch typos and 0 of 160 real-world misspellings across English and Dutch. With every tuning slider at its most aggressive value it corrected 2.5 to 2.9% of typos, and about a quarter of those corrections picked the wrong word. The suggestion bar does better: the intended word is the first suggestion for 60 to 81% of typos, depending on the set.

The causes are in the scoring and data, not in the thresholds a user can tune. The details follow.

## Method

`baseline-harness/engine.js` is a line-by-line JavaScript port of `LatinLanguageProvider.suggest()` and `HighCertaintyAutocorrectPolicy`: candidate generation (prefix index, distance-1 delete index over the top 20,000 words), `rankSuggestionCandidate`, `calculateConfidence`, `MixedLanguageScoringPolicy`, and the app-profile threshold math. It loads the shipped `en_50k.txt` and `nl_50k.txt` assets. It leaves out the user dictionary and personal n-grams, which matches a fresh install. Personal context adds at most +0.10 confidence, which does not change any conclusion below.

The harness also contains a reference noisy-channel scorer: log frequency prior, weighted edit distance with QWERTY key-adjacency costs, candidates up to edit distance 2 for words of 7+ letters, and the literal input competing as "keep what I typed". It has no context model and no tuning. It is there to estimate headroom, not as the proposed final design.

Datasets:

- Synthetic touch typos: words sampled from the top 10,000 of each dictionary, either weighted by usage frequency or uniformly from ranks 100 to 10,000. One error per word: adjacent-key substitution 55%, omission 20%, insertion 15%, transposition 10%. Typos that happen to form another dictionary word are counted separately and left out of the correction sets.
- Real-world misspellings: 100 English (`teh`, `becuase`, `recieve`, `thnaks`, ...), 14 English missing-apostrophe forms (`dont`, `im`, ...), 60 Dutch (`mischien`, `eigenlik`, `bedakt`, `vergaderign`, ...).
- Legitimate out-of-dictionary words: 57 names, product names, jargon and Dutch chat shorthand (`Ownkey`, `Voxtral`, `webhook`, `deployen`, `idd`, `wss`, `zsm`, ...).

Reproduce with `node --max-old-space-size=8192 bench.js <path-to>/app/src/main/assets/ime/dict/frequencywords` from the harness folder. `maxslider.js` takes the same argument.

Caveat: the synthetic typos use the same key-adjacency model as the reference scorer, so its synthetic numbers are optimistic. The real-world lists do not have that problem.

## Root causes found in code and data

1. Confidence cannot reach the threshold. `calculateConfidence` is `0.55 * frequency/maxFrequency + 0.25 * distanceTerm + 0.15 * prefix + 0.05 * lengthCloseness`, using linear raw counts. "you" has 28.8M hits in `en_50k.txt` and the 100th word has 1.17M, so the frequency term is below 0.03 for almost every target word. A distance-1 correction tops out at 0.79 before language blending and 0.81 after, against a default threshold of 0.88. In chat apps (threshold 0.79) only corrections to "you" can pass, and in Dutch the chat profile can never fire. Lowering the sliders lets through only corrections toward the most frequent words, whatever the typo looked like. Log-scaling the frequency term does not fix this on its own: the ceiling holds even at frequency 1.0, and a log-frequency version of the same formula still fires 0% at defaults (review re-run, below).
2. Typo shape is ignored. Every substitution costs the same, so `qhat` is as close to "that" as to "what". Tap coordinates never reach the NLP layer: `SuggestionProvider.suggest()` only receives `EditorContent` text. Only glide typing uses key geometry.
3. The dictionaries are raw subtitle frequency lists. `en_50k.txt` splits contractions into separate tokens (`'t` 9.6M, `'s` 14.3M) and stores some with backticks (`` don`t ``), so none of 14 common contractions exist as words while all 14 apostrophe-less forms (`dont`, `im`, `youre`) count as correct. Known misspellings are valid words (`untill`, `seperate`, `occured`, `tommorow`, `alot`). OCR artifacts are high-frequency words (`l` 126k, `s`, `o`, `t`). `nl_50k.txt` has no apostrophes, so `z'n` and `m'n` cannot be produced. The older `data.json` did contain "don't" and "i'm" (next to `dont` and `im`); switching to FrequencyWords lost them. Dutch misspellings are valid words too: `mischien` (1,043 hits) and `eigelijk` (286) are valid words, so they can never be corrected. 13 of the 100 English and 5 of the 60 Dutch real-world misspellings are dictionary words.
4. No context model. Next-word and correction ranking only use personal n-grams (empty on a fresh install) and words already in the current field. The engine cannot choose between "then" and "than" or fix real-word errors.
5. Short words are skipped. Typo lookup needs 4+ letters (`MinLengthForTypoCorrections`) and auto-commit needs 4+ letters by default, so `teh`, `hte` and `adn` are never corrected. For `teh` the top suggestion is "tehran". Setting the minimum length to 3 in the tuning screen does nothing, because the 4-letter lookup limit is hard-coded.
6. Candidate truncation, minor. Per language, prefix completions fill the 16-slot candidate map first, and if they fill it at most one typo correction gets in. In practice this happens for 0.0% of English and 0.1% of Dutch typos of 4+ letters (review re-run), so it is a code smell, not a cause.
7. Completion reach. Completion from a 5-letter prefix reaches 53% top-3 versus 80% for a frequency oracle. The prefix index keeps only the 48 most frequent words per 3-letter prefix, but deepening it alone makes things worse (46.9% at depth 5), because the length penalty dominates when linear frequency is near zero. The ranking is the main problem.
8. Stale candidates on space. `NlpManager.suggest()` runs on `Dispatchers.Default`, and space commits whatever candidate list finished last. Candidates do not record which input they were computed for. A fast typist can press space before the list for the final letter exists. Not yet measured on device.
9. The quick-action autocorrect toggle is a placeholder (`KeyboardManager.handleToggleAutocorrect` shows "not yet implemented"). The `highCertaintyAutocorrectEnabled` pref it needs already exists and the policy honors it.
10. Completions can never autocorrect. Prefix completions enter the candidate map first with distance 0, and `putIfAbsent` keeps that entry when the same word comes back as a distance-1 correction. The policy requires distance 1 or more, so a dropped final letter (`becaus` for "because") is never fixed.
11. Wrong trigger characters. `KeyboardManager` auto-commits on any non-alphabetic character, including apostrophe, hyphen, digits and `@`. Harmless today because nothing is eligible. Once scoring works, `isn` would be corrected before the apostrophe of "isn't" is typed, and e-mail addresses and URLs typed in chat apps would be exposed. Enter never autocorrects (`handleEnter`).

## Independent review, 2026-09-24

A second reviewer re-ran the harness, reproduced every number above exactly, and checked the port against the Kotlin code: candidate generation, the 16-slot map and its break logic, rank score, confidence, language weights and blending, app-profile math, the top-8 cut in `NlpManager`, and the runner-up gap all match. Divergences that do not change the headline:

- The harness passes no context tokens. The app passes preceding words plus the input itself, which only changes NL+EN weighting.
- The personal n-gram boost is below 0.10 (`continuationScore = c/(2+c)`). Even at its maximum, the default threshold needs a target at 93% of the top word's frequency, which only "you" meets.
- User-dictionary entries only block autocorrect. They are only offered as suggestions in `spell()`.
- Glide typing commits through `commitGesture`, not auto-commit. Clipboard suggestions replace the word list and suppress autocorrect.
- The legacy `data.json` fallback also gives 0% at defaults.

The reviewer's experiments changed the plan:

| Experiment | EN synthetic right / wrong | NL synthetic right / wrong | EN real right |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Noisy-channel scoring on the current candidate indexes, 3-letter minimum | 59.3% / 1.2% | 44.8% / 0.8% | 41.0% |
| Reference scorer (full vocabulary, distance 2) | 58.9% / 1.2% | 44.7% / 0.8% | 41.0% |
| Reference with a lower unknown-word prior (log 30) | 77.4% / 1.5% | 68.0% / 1.0% | 59% |

- Candidate generation is not the bottleneck: removing the 20,000-word cap and adding distance 2 changed nothing on these sets. Scoring is where the gain is.
- The lower unknown-word prior raises recall, but it changes 3 of the 57 legitimate out-of-dictionary words and drops precision on the vocabulary-uniform set to 91%. The reference with the default prior already scores only 59% precision on that set (2.2% right, 1.5% wrong). Precision on rare target words is the binding constraint, and calibration has to be done on non-circular data.
